# Geo

A geographical report starts with the measure, the reporting period and the boundary definition. Colouring regions or drawing connections comes after those facts are clear. Otherwise an attractive map can imply comparisons the data does not support.

## Regional measures

Use [capacity](./map-capacity-regions), [coverage](./map-coverage-score) or [service reliability](./map-service-health) when a denominator is part of the question. Keep missing denominators distinct from zero. [Sales](./map-sales-territory) retain exact monetary amounts and the territory definition used for attribution.

The actual [choropleth Compose component](/reference/charts/geographic-and-route/map-choropleth/) takes supplied GeoJSON and joins values by stable IDs. Validate geometry and records before rendering; a map-type string does not fetch a boundary file.

## Connections and overlays

[Courier legs](./lines-courier-routes), [supply quantities](./lines-supply-routes) and [network rates](./lines-network-traffic) use actual endpoint connections. Their curves are schematic: they do not calculate roads, distances, journey times or a navigation route.

Map and line series currently fit their bounds independently. Consequently, pages with overlay titles describe that limitation and use [paired region/connection views](/reference/charts/geo-and-route/geo-network/) instead. They do not promise a correctly aligned geographic overlay; that needs a shared projection outside these examples.

## Read the result as evidence

Translate visible captions and keep units, reporting windows and freshness explicit. Offer exact values in a table or list and use colour palettes that remain useful without red/green discrimination. Load snapshots through effects and reducers, not during widget conversion.

Test joins, missing records, invalid ratios and report replacement before visual checks. Gallery screenshots are separate fixtures, not qualification of live data, routes or an operational planning workflow.
